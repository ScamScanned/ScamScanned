How the verdict gets built

This page documents, in plain language, exactly what the banner is allowed to say, what evidence it may use, and the limits we state out loud. The code that does all of it is public — nothing on this page is a promise the source cannot back.

Rule zero: the banner reports patterns, never verdicts about people. It never uses labels that assert what a listing, seller, or review is. It says what printed signals matched and in which band. When evidence contradicts a weaker inference — for instance, a seller record with thousands of positive ratings against an odd-looking storefront name — the weaker signal is dropped. Suppressing a warning is an honest decline, not a stamp of approval.

What the extension reads

  • Printed seller history: the feedback cluster shown in the buy box ("N ratings, P% positive") and platform badges such as "New seller" / "New to Amazon".
  • Printed logistics: delivery windows, lead-time phrasing, and fulfillment claims — including the case where an Amazon-fulfillment claim sits next to a weeks-long delivery window, a pair of printed statements that cannot both be true.
  • Printed trade signals: the list-price/current price pair, the marketplace's own item return-frequency flags, and the offer's checkout structure.
  • Listing text surfaces: the title and URL the page itself chose — mass-generation artifacts like non-native punctuation or keyword-stacking patterns.
  • What it never touches: your account, other tabs, your browsing history, or anything you type. It only reads text printed on the page in front of you.

Amazon's four bands

This scale applies to Amazon. Walmart, eBay, and Etsy currently use the simpler red/blue banner.

Silent — no banner

No configured check matched the page. That is the honest state, not a certificate.

Low caution — yellow

Exactly one soft signal (for example, only a strange storefront name) with nothing stacked behind it. This band is usually fine; the fast check is optional.

Elevated — red

One strong signal alone, or a stack of weaker ones. Example: a third-party fulfillment window weeks past marketplace norms, whatever the seller record says.

Extreme — bright red

Several strong signals align, or a strong signal carries multiple corroborators — for example, a brand-new seller with a weeks-long delivery window and no Amazon handling. The advice here is to pick a different listing.

Amazon's six meter zones

On Amazon's yellow, red, and blue banners, the meter at the top lights one zone per family of checks — several individual rules collapse into the same zone. Lit means at least one rule in that family fired; it counts, it does not grade.

The shipping-cost check is a cost disclosure, not a scam inference: it only fires when the page prints an explicit shipping charge over $5, with stronger emphasis as the charge rises. A lone extended-shipping warning is yellow at 14–27 days and red at 28+ days.

  • Seller record — feedback-share breaches, sparse-history storefronts, listings with zero third-party reviews.
  • Seller name — storefront-name shape inferences (the weakest family, and the only one another printed record can suppress).
  • Price — deep list-price bands, stacked discount corroborators, and a printed shipping charge over $5.
  • Fulfillment & checkout — long delivery horizons, missing standard buy box, and fulfillment-claim conflicts.
  • Listing text — mass-generation artifacts.
  • Platform return data — the marketplace's own frequently-returned flag.

Why the Amazon banner shows both sides

On Amazon, every banner pairs Watch for (what matched) with What's on your side (observations that point the other way: a large printed buyer-review count, a standard checkout flow, the absence of a platform defect flag, no mass-generation artifacts). Both columns are page facts. On the extreme tier, the counterweight column is hidden, because a verdict built on several aligned signals should not be softened mid-paragraph.

Limits we state out loud

We read printed text only. The extension does not analyze product photos, review images, or review text; the banner points you at the fastest human check instead. Heuristics age. Marketplaces reprint their pages constantly; a printed format we parse today can change shape without notice. Parse failure means silence. If a page half-renders or the text moves, no banner appears — we never emit a warning on uncertain reads. We are not affiliated with Amazon, Walmart, eBay, Etsy, Craigslist, or Facebook. The marketplace names appear only as the pages the extension is registered to examine. Enforcement notices, when they appear, summarize public records published by governments and courts — never a ScamScanned determination.

What data moves, and when

On Amazon and Walmart, when a warning banner actually fires, one anonymous diagnostic goes to ScamScanned's own server — the marketplace, the listing address, the page title, and the seller name the page itself printed. No accounts, no cookies, no identifiers, nothing sold or shared for advertising. eBay, Etsy, Craigslist and Facebook scans send nothing at all. The full data policy lives at privacy.html. Everything the tool claims about itself is checkable — read the source, and if any request exists beyond the one diagnostic, that's a bug worth reporting publicly.

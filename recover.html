ScamScanned Recovery Guide

Get your money back

The order never shipped. Or it arrived unlike the listing, or the seller went quiet mid-refund. This is the order to work in, the real deadlines, and the moves that can close a door if you make them too early. Free, no email gate, no upsell.

Rule zero: find the deadline before you argue. A missed deadline can end a claim even when the seller is wrong. The order page may not show the claim window; the platform's help page does. Write down the latest estimated delivery date and the claim deadline today, then follow the ladder below.

First, check who sold it and how you paid

The wrong lane wastes the deadline. Check the order confirmation before you file anything.

  • Amazon: if the order says Sold by Amazon.com, use the normal return/refund flow. If a third-party seller sold and shipped it, use the A-to-z Guarantee. If it is a Marketplace item delivered using Prime, Amazon says to contact customer service instead.
  • Walmart: items sold by Walmart use Walmart returns. Items sold by a Marketplace seller use the seller first, then Walmart Customer Care under the Marketplace Promise.
  • eBay and Etsy: the seller is the other party, but the platform's buyer-protection process is the lane that can actually refund you.
  • Facebook Marketplace: only purchases made with shipping or checkout on Facebook/Meta Pay are covered. Local pickup, Messenger deals, and third-party payment links are not.
  • Payment method: a credit card gives you the strongest card-dispute route. Debit cards are weaker. Gift cards, wire transfers, and cryptocurrency are usually not recoverable.

The short version

  1. Open the order and start the platform's own problem/return flow. Do not open a card dispute first.
  2. Message the seller inside the platform with the facts. Keep it short and unemotional.
  3. Wait out the platform's response window (usually 2 to 3 business days, sometimes 48 hours).
  4. Escalate to the platform claim before the deadline, not after.
  5. Use the card/payment lane only if the platform lane is denied, closed, or past its window.
  6. Report the listing separately. Reporting can help enforcement; it is not a refund.

Step one: build the record

  1. Save the order facts: order confirmation, date, amount, seller name, item, and the latest estimated delivery date you were shown.
  2. Snapshot the listing with the web address and date visible, plus the price, delivery promise, and seller name. Listings get edited or deleted; your copy does not.
  3. Keep the thread: every platform message, tracking update, photo of the damaged or wrong item, and return receipt.
  4. Write a five-line chronology: order date, amount, promised-by date, what actually happened, and today's date. Facts only.

Amazon: the third-party ladder

1. Ask the seller first, through the order

Open Your Orders, choose Problem with order, and send a refund/return request to the seller. Amazon requires you to have contacted the seller more than 48 hours before an A-to-z claim. Keep the message factual: order number, what was promised, what happened, and what you want. Note the date and time you sent it. Do not close a claim on a promise; close it when the refund posts or the platform says the case is resolved.

2. Check the A-to-z eligibility before you file

The order must be for physical goods sold and fulfilled by a third-party seller. Amazon's published condition is that the order's last possible delivery date is 90 days or less. Then:

  • Never arrived: 3 days have passed since the latest estimated delivery date (or tracking shows delivery, whichever comes first), and you contacted the seller more than 48 hours ago without a resolution.
  • Damaged, defective, or materially different: you requested a return more than 48 hours ago and the seller has not authorized it.

File from the order page. Amazon says claims can take up to a week. You can file up to 90 days after the estimated delivery date. If the claim is denied, Amazon's appeal window is 30 calendar days from the decision. The current eligibility page is Amazon's A-to-z Guarantee help page.

Not covered: digital items, services, stored-value instruments, Spot Buys, automobiles, and items sold or shipped by Amazon itself.

3. Do not run the card dispute and A-to-z at the same time

Amazon says that if you request a refund or chargeback from your payment provider, you are not eligible for an A-to-z refund on that order. Pick the platform lane first. If A-to-z is denied or closed, ask your card issuer what remains available.

4. If the item arrives while the claim is open

Update the claim immediately; do not let a non-delivery claim sit while tracking shows delivered. If the item arrived as described and you no longer need the claim, close or cancel it (or ask customer service to close it) and use the normal return/refund path if you still want to return it. If it arrived damaged or materially different, tell the platform and start the return/refund reason that matches. Keep the item, packaging, and inserts; photograph the condition before you send anything back.

The card lane: second, not first

Use this after the platform claim is denied, closed, or past its window. If it was denied, read the reason and use any appeal the platform allows first; a denial is not automatically the end of every option, but do not reopen the same claim without new evidence. The card clocks are separate from marketplace clocks, and the rules depend on the card type.

Credit card

For a charge you did not receive as agreed, the federal billing-error process applies. Send a written dispute to the address on your statement for billing errors; it must reach the issuer within 60 days after the first statement showing the charge. Keep a copy. The issuer must acknowledge it within 30 days and resolve it within two billing cycles or 90 days. You can withhold the disputed amount while it investigates, but keep paying the undisputed part. If the issuer credits you temporarily, that credit can be reversed if it decides against you. The CFPB's billing-dispute guide walks through the written-notice requirement.

For a quality or “not as described” dispute rather than non-delivery, ask the issuer about a card-network dispute; the billing-error rules are not the only route, but the evidence requirements are different.

Debit card

Debit protections are narrower and the reporting clock is tighter. Call the number on the card and ask about a dispute or any voluntary protection, then follow up in writing. Do not wait for the platform claim to finish if the debit window is about to close; ask the bank what it needs.

What to send the issuer

The order confirmation, the listing snapshot, the delivery promise, the platform case number and outcome, the seller messages, tracking, and your five-line chronology. Ask for the dispute case number and the date the issuer received your written notice.

Gift cards, wire transfers, and cryptocurrency are usually not recoverable. Report them, but do not pay a “recovery” service that promises to get the money back.

Other marketplaces: the exact windows

Walmart Marketplace

Contact the seller from the order first; sellers are expected to respond within 2 business days. If that fails, Walmart Customer Care applies the Marketplace Promise: 2+ days past the expected delivery date, refund requests accepted up to 30 days after the last estimated delivery date, tracking showing the item never shipped, a different item the seller refused to take back, or a tracked return with no refund within 2 business days. Eligible Marketplace items can also be returned in a Walmart store within 30 days. Official Marketplace Promise.

eBay

Report an item that never arrived within 30 calendar days after the estimated or actual delivery date. The seller has 3 business days to respond; if they do not resolve it, ask eBay to step in. eBay may step in automatically when there is no valid tracking. For a return, request it within 30 days of delivery; the seller has 3 business days, and a refund is due within 2 business days after the returned item arrives. Appeals are 30 days. Official Money Back Guarantee.

Etsy

Send a Help with order message to the seller. You can open a case after the maximum estimated delivery date has passed and at least 48 hours after that message; for a non-delivery case, wait 7+ days past the maximum estimated delivery date. Open the case within 30 days after the estimated delivery date. Etsy Purchase Protection can cover non-delivery, damage, a materially different item, or a delivery 7+ days late. If you paid through PayPal instead of Etsy Payments, handle the dispute through PayPal. Official case guide.

PayPal purchases

Open the dispute in the Resolution Center within 180 days of the payment for an item that never arrived. For “significantly not as described,” the window is 30 days after delivery or 180 days after payment, whichever is sooner. Escalate to a claim within 20 days after opening the dispute, or it closes automatically. PayPal may ask you to wait at least 7 days from the payment before escalating. If you also dispute the charge with your card issuer, PayPal closes its dispute. Official Purchase Protection.

Facebook Marketplace

Only orders paid through checkout with shipping on Facebook/Meta Pay are covered. Local pickup, Messenger transactions, and payments through third-party sites are not. File the claim from the order; if approved, the refund covers the item price and shipping. Official Purchase Protection.

Craigslist and cash trades

There is no platform purchase protection. Meet in person, inspect before paying, and do not prepay or send money transfers. If you paid off-platform with a card, the card dispute may be your only route, but it is weaker without a platform order record.

Report it — but know what reporting does

  • The marketplace: use the report controls on the listing or order. Reports can contribute to enforcement, but they do not refund your order by themselves.
  • The FTC (US): reportfraud.ftc.gov. The FTC cannot resolve an individual report or get your money back. It shares reports with more than 2,000 law-enforcement partners and uses them to investigate patterns.
  • The FBI's IC3: ic3.gov. The IC3 does not investigate individual complaints or recover funds, and it will not contact you. Complaints may be referred to law enforcement.
  • Card or bank issue: you can also file a complaint with the CFPB.
  • State route: your state attorney general or local consumer-protection office is another complaint channel; it is not a fast refund lane.

Beware the second wave. After a bad purchase, messages often arrive claiming to be “recovery agents” who can retrieve your funds for a fee, or “support” calling from a number you did not contact and asking for card details, gift cards, crypto, or remote access. No legitimate recovery starts with a cold contact asking for money. The FTC and IC3 do not call to demand payment, and the IC3 does not work with fee-based recovery services. A seller who asks you to cancel a claim or pay a fee to release a refund is also a red flag. Real recovery is the ladder above, started by you, on the platform's own pages.

A field pattern worth learning

A reported case: a listing with professional-looking photos, a normal delivery promise, and no review history. The order never entered the carrier network. The photos were not the proof; the missing seller history and the order that never moved were. Treat polished photos as neutral. Check who is selling, what the page prints about the seller, and whether tracking actually moves.
